Large software companies are moving AI agents out of pilot programs and into production workflows, according to a survey of 200 engineering leaders. The most common blocker was not model quality but the lack of repeatable evaluation: teams said they could not prove an agent was safe to ship without regression tests for multi-step behavior.

Several companies described new internal roles that sit between engineering and policy. These program owners define evaluation gates, coordinate red teaming, and keep launch documentation ready for AI governance reviews. One director said every agent launch now needs a written risk assessment and a rollback plan before it reaches customers.

Observability is the second gap. Agents call tools and other services, so teams are extending tracing and incident processes to cover agent actions, not just API latency. Leaders expect hiring for people who can run these launches across engineering, legal, and product to grow through 2027.
